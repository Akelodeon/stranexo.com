Client case

Initial situation: Within a manufacturing company, import operations management was scattered across several teams (accounting, procurement, warehouse, Supply Chain assistant), with no dedicated function and no consolidated view.

Diagnostic: No centralized import expertise, a scattered organization, unidentified extra costs.

Actions: Built an Import function, an Export function and a Transport function from the ground up. The Export function covered international sales strategy, structuring commercial offers, defining the right Incoterms for each target country, and regulatory analysis of target markets to secure entry into new markets. The Transport function covered negotiating air, road and ocean freight, for both import and export (pallet, parcel, integrators, international multimodal).

Results: Average margin improvement of 40% on international transport (FCL, LCL, air, rail) and 25% on LTL/FTL, across all import and export flows, measured over 12 months. Secured entry into new export markets through structured commercial offers and regulatory analysis of target countries.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Answers to the main questions about Fractional Supply Chain / Logistics / Transport Leadership.

Do you act as a freight forwarder?

No. I work only in advisory and management support. Your company remains the sole principal and direct contracting party with the carriers.

What's the difference between Fractional Leadership and a consultant?

A consultant typically works on a specific issue or a one-off assignment. Fractional Leadership supports your company over the long run, with a global view of your organization in order to structure it, coordinate it and help it progress.

What's the difference with the Transport expertise?

Transport is a targeted assignment aimed at analyzing a situation and supporting the implementation of improvement levers. Fractional Leadership consists of supporting the management of your organization over the long run, a few days a month.

Do you work full-time?

No. The principle is to adapt the time commitment to your company's real needs. A few days a month is often enough to bring leadership-level expertise, while keeping a high degree of flexibility.

Do you make the decisions instead of the company?

No. My role is to bring an outside view, contribute to the thinking, facilitate trade-offs and support the implementation of decisions. Strategic choices remain with your leadership team.
